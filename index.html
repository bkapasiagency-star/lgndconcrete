Be honest

Your driveway is the first thing people see. What's it saying?

Illinois winters are brutal on concrete. Freeze, thaw, salt, repeat. This is a real driveway we were called out to. Tap the gold markers to see what was going on.

1

Cracks running the full width

Once a crack opens, water gets under the slab, freezes and pushes it apart a little more every winter. It never closes back up.

How we fix itFull tear-out, a compacted stone base and steel reinforcement, with control joints cut so the slab moves where we planned.
2

A surface that's flaking away

Pitting and exposed stone mean the top layer has given up. It holds water, stains easily and gets worse every time salt hits it.

How we fix itA proper mix for our climate, finished at the right time and sealed, so the surface stays tight.
3

A patch on top of a problem

That lighter strip by the garage is an old repair. Patches hide the symptom. The base underneath is still moving.

How we fix itWe deal with what's under the concrete: grading, base and drainage. Then we pour once.
4

Edges breaking down

When the edge of a drive crumbles, the lawn creeps in, the slab loses support and the whole thing starts to look tired.

How we fix itClean formed edges, proper thickness all the way out, and the yard graded back to meet it.

How we build

What you can't see is what makes it last.

Anyone can make concrete look good on day one. Scroll and watch what goes under an LGND slab.

Existing yard

Tear-out and excavation

Old concrete, pavers and soft soil come out. We dig down to solid ground, not just far enough to hide the problem.

Base prep

Stone goes in and gets compacted. This is the layer that stops sinking and settling, and it's the first place cheap bids cut.

Grading and framing

Forms are set to line and to slope, so water runs away from your house and every edge comes out crisp.

Reinforcement

Steel is tied in before the truck shows up. It holds the slab together through every freeze and thaw.

The pour

The right mix, placed and leveled by a crew that has done this for years. No rushing it and no watering it down.

Finishing

Broomed, bordered, stamped or colored. Joints cut clean, edges tooled, site cleaned up. This is the part you get to show off.
